Government Contracting

A SAM.gov registration checklist for new small-business contractors

Registering in SAM.gov is the price of entry for federal work, and it is free. It is also where new contractors lose the most time to avoidable mistakes. Here is the sequence, the prerequisites, and the pitfalls.

Apex Gama Defense Solutions · 8 min read

The System for Award Management (SAM.gov) is the federal government's registry of entities eligible to do business with it. No registration, no award — and for most small businesses the process is more tedious than hard. It is also free: the government never charges for a SAM registration, and the help is available directly. The checklist below walks the sequence a new small business should follow, with the steps that most often cause delay flagged.

Before you start: prerequisites

  • A legally formed business entity, with your legal name and physical address exactly as they appear on your formation documents and tax records.
  • Your Taxpayer Identification Number (EIN) as issued by the IRS, matched to your legal name.
  • A business bank account for electronic payment information.
  • A Login.gov account for the person who will manage the registration.

The single most common cause of delay is a mismatch between the name and address you enter and the official records those are validated against. Make them identical, character for character, before you begin.

1. Get your Unique Entity ID (UEI)

The government retired the DUNS number in 2022. Entities are now identified by a Unique Entity ID (UEI) that is requested and assigned within SAM.gov itself. If you only need a UEI — for example, to receive a subaward — you can request one without completing a full registration. For prime contracting, you will complete the full registration, which issues the UEI as part of the process.

2. Pass entity validation

SAM.gov validates your entity's legal name and address against authoritative records before it will let you register. If your details match, this is quick. If they do not — because your formation documents, IRS records, and what you typed disagree — you will have to submit documentation to resolve it, and this is the step that most often stalls a new registration for days or weeks. Get it right the first time by using your exact legal records.

3. Complete the core registration

  • Entity information: legal name, physical and mailing address, business start date, and fiscal year.
  • Points of contact: the people authorized to act on the registration.
  • Banking / financial information: for electronic funds transfer.
  • NAICS codes: your primary and additional codes (choose these deliberately — they drive which solicitations you see and which size standard you are measured against).

4. Work through the representations and certifications

The reps and certs are a long series of statements about your business — size status, socioeconomic categories, compliance attestations, and more — that become binding when you bid. Answer them carefully and truthfully. This is also where your small-business status and any socioeconomic designations are represented; do not claim a status you do not hold or cannot document. If a certification requires a separate program application (certain set-aside programs do), the SAM reps and certs are not a substitute for that application.

5. Submit and wait for activation

Once submitted, the registration goes through processing — including IRS and other validations — before it becomes active. A new registration is not instantaneous; plan for processing time and do not assume you can bid the day you submit. You can monitor status in your SAM.gov account.

6. Keep it active: renew annually

A SAM registration expires and must be renewed every year to stay active. An expired registration can make you ineligible for award and can interrupt payment, so treat the renewal date as a hard deadline and update the record whenever your information changes — address, banking, points of contact, NAICS, or size status.

Pitfalls to avoid

  • Paying a third party. Registration is free. Companies that solicit you to “register for you” for a fee are selling something the government provides at no cost and with free help.
  • Name/address mismatches. The leading cause of entity-validation delays. Match your legal records exactly.
  • Treating reps and certs as a formality. They are binding representations; errors can have real consequences when you bid.
  • Letting it lapse. Mark the renewal date now; an inactive registration undoes the work.

A note on accuracy

SAM.gov's interface and steps change over time, and the authoritative source is always SAM.gov itself and its Federal Service Desk. Use this checklist to prepare and to understand the sequence; confirm the current steps and requirements on SAM.gov before and during registration.

This article is general information for new government contractors, not legal or procurement advice. SAM.gov processes and requirements change; the authoritative source is SAM.gov and the Federal Service Desk. Registration is free — the government does not charge for it.
